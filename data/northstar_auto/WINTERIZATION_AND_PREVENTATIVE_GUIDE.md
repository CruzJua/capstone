# SEASONAL VEHICLE WINTERIZATION & PREVENTATIVE GUIDE

**NorthStar Auto Care** — *Precision Diagnostics. Honest Service. Road-Ready Reliability.*

Cold weather finds weak parts. A winter check-up before the first hard freeze is the cheapest repair you will ever make.

## 1. Cold Cranking Amps (CCA) Battery Health

Lead-acid and AGM automotive batteries lose up to **50%** of their cranking power at temperatures below 32°F (0°C), while your engine needs more power to crank, since the oil is thicker.

- **Conductance Testing:** A handheld conductance tester sends a small signal through the battery and compares the result with the battery's rated CCA, and it does so without draining it. Batteries testing below **70% rated CCA** are flagged for proactive replacement before sub-zero temperatures hit.
- **Example:** A battery rated at 600 CCA that tests at 380 CCA (63%) is flagged for replacement.
- **Test Conditions:** The battery should be at 40°F–100°F (4°C–38°C) and have at least a 12.4 V resting voltage. A battery with a low charge is charged first, and then retested.
- **Terminal Corrosion:** Application of dielectric grease and terminal cleaning to prevent voltage drops. Look for white or blue-green powder at the terminals.
- **Age:** Most batteries last 3 to 5 years. We recommend testing every year after year 3.
- **Parked Vehicles:** If a vehicle sits more than 2 weeks, use a maintainer.

## 2. Coolant Freeze-Point & Hydrometer Testing

- **Concentration Target:** 50/50 ethylene glycol and deionized water mixture providing freeze protection down to **-34°F (-37°C)**.
- **Testing:** A hydrometer or refractometer measures the density of the coolant, and the reading is converted to a freeze point. We test it when the engine is cool.
  - **Pass:** -34°F (-37°C) or lower.
  - **Marginal:** -20°F to -33°F (-29°C to -36°C), so we top off with proper mix or flush the system.
  - **Fail:** Warmer than -20°F (-29°C). Flush and refill.
- **Don't Overconcentrate:** A mix richer than 70% glycol freezes at a *higher* temperature and transfers heat less effectively. Use pre-mixed or deionized water, and never tap water.
- **Boil-over & Corrosion Protection:** Fresh inhibitor additives prevent internal engine block scale and heater core pinhole corrosion. A 50/50 mix also raises the boiling point to about 265°F (129°C) with a 15 psi cap.
- **Coolant Types:** Use the type your manufacturer requires (OAT, HOAT, or IAT) and don't mix types, because they can gel. Typical service interval is 30,000 to 60,000 miles for extended-life coolants (see `service_intervals_matrix.json`).

## 3. Tire Compound & Winter Tread Depth Metrics

- **Winter Tread Minimum:** All-season tires require a minimum of **4/32 inch** tread depth for snow traction (replacing normal 2/32 inch legal limit).
- **How to Check:** Insert a quarter upside down with Washington's head into the groove. If the top of his head is visible, you are below 4/32 inch. We measure all four tires with a depth gauge during the DVI.
- **Compound Glass Transition:** All-season rubber compounds harden below 45°F (7°C), significantly increasing braking distances. Dedicated winter tires recommended for sub-freezing regional travel.
- **Winter Tires:** Look for the three-peak mountain snowflake symbol. Install a full set of four, and never just two.
- **Tire Pressure:** Pressure drops about **1 psi for every 10°F (5.5°C)** drop in temperature. Check monthly with the tires cold, and set to the pressure on the driver-door placard.

## 4. Salt Corrosion Prevention

- **Wash:** Rinse the vehicle every **10 to 14 days** during salt season, and after any storm, particularly the undercarriage, wheel wells, and rocker panels. Wash when temperatures are above 32°F (0°C) so the doors and locks don't freeze.
- **Inspect:** Brake lines, fuel lines, and suspension components rust first. These are checked in the DVI.
- **Protect:** Undercoating or wax-based rust inhibitors in the fall protect exposed metal, and a coat of wax on the paint protects the finish.
- **Dry:** Lubricate door seals with silicone spray to prevent freezing. Wipe door jambs.
- **Brake Components:** Salt corrodes calipers and slide pins, so we clean and lubricate them at each brake service.

## 5. Other Cold-Weather Checks

| Item | What We Do | Why |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Engine oil | Check viscosity grade (0W-20 or 5W-30 full synthetic as specified) | Thick oil strains the starter |
| Wiper blades | Replace if streaking, and use winter-rated washer fluid rated to -20°F (-29°C) | Visibility |
| Brake fluid | Test moisture content, replace if above 3% | Moisture freezes and corrodes |
| Belts and hoses | Inspect for cracks | Cold makes rubber brittle |
| Heater and defroster | Test output | Safety |

## 6. Winter Emergency Kit

Blanket, flashlight, jumper cables, ice scraper, small shovel, sand or kitty litter for traction, phone charger, water, and snacks. Keep your fuel tank at least **half full** at all times in winter.
