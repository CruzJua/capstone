# LEATHER & WAXED CANVAS CARE GUIDE

**Iron & Oak Provision Co.** — *Built for the Trail. Proven for a Lifetime.*

Good gear lasts if you treat it right. None of this takes long.

## 1. Vegetable-Tanned Leather Maintenance

- **Biannual Conditioning:** Apply natural beeswax or neatsfoot oil balm every **6 months** to prevent dry cracking.
  - Dry climates (under 30% relative humidity): condition every **3 to 4 months**.
  - Humid climates (over 70%): condition every 6 months, and watch for mildew.
- **How to Condition:**
  1. Brush off dirt and dust.
  2. Wipe with a damp cloth and let it dry for 1 hour at room temperature.
  3. Work a **dime-sized amount** of balm in with a clean cloth, using small circles.
  4. Let it absorb for 12 hours, then buff off the excess.
  - Don't over-oil. Too much oil softens the leather and makes it stretch.
- **Cleaning Protocol:** Wipe with a damp cloth. Use saddle soap sparingly for deep mud or grease removal, and condition afterward.
- **Prohibitions:** *Strictly no machine washing or heat drying.* High temperature strips natural oils and distorts the grain. Keep leather away from radiators, wood stoves, and car dashboards.
- **If It Gets Soaked:** Blot with a towel, stuff boots or belts with paper if needed, and dry slowly at room temperature, for **24 to 48 hours**, out of direct sun. Condition after it is dry.
- **Patina:** Darkening, scratches, and creases are normal. Veg-tan leather ages to a deeper color the more you use it.
- **Hardware:** Solid brass tarnishes. A little tarnish is fine. To clean it, use a dry cloth. Avoid chemical polishes on leather.

## 2. Waxed Canvas Care

### Routine Cleaning
- Brush off dry mud and dust with a soft brush.
- Spot clean with **cold water** and a soft brush or sponge. Do not use soap, detergent, or solvents, because they strip the wax.
- Hang to air dry, away from direct heat.
- **Never** machine wash, dry clean, or tumble dry.

### Waxed Canvas Rewaxing Procedure
Rewax once a year, or seasonally for heavy use, or whenever water stops beading.

1. **Clean:** Remove debris using cold water and a soft brush. Let the canvas dry completely.
2. **Apply:** Rub a paraffin/beeswax bar evenly across the canvas weave using circular motions. Focus on seams, shoulders, elbows, and pocket flaps. Use about **one 3 oz bar per coat**.
3. **Melt:** Use a heat gun on its low setting or a hairdryer on medium heat, held **4 to 6 inches (10 to 15 cm)** from the fabric, to melt the wax into the fibers. Keep it moving. Do not exceed about **150°F (65°C)** at the fabric surface.
4. **Even Out:** Wipe off any pooling wax with a clean cloth while it's warm.
5. **Cure:** Allow the canvas to cure flat or hanging in a cool, dry area for **24 hours**.

Test on a hidden area first. Fresh wax may darken the canvas slightly.

## 3. Wool Overshirt & Field Pants

- **Wool Overshirts:** Spot clean when possible. When you must wash, hand wash in cold water (under 86°F / 30°C) with wool-safe detergent, then lay flat to dry. No tumble drying, because it shrinks wool.
- **Field Pants (canvas):** Wash cold, inside out, on gentle, and hang dry. Washing will gradually remove wax from waxed finishes, so rewax afterward.

## 4. Off-Season Storage

- Store gear in **breathable canvas storage bags** in well-ventilated spaces. Avoid plastic bags and sealed containers.
- Keep the temperature between 50°F and 75°F (10°C and 24°C) and relative humidity under 60% where possible.
- Avoid direct UV sunlight, which fades and dries both canvas and leather, and avoid damp basements, which promote mold growth and leather degradation.
- Store coats on a wide hanger. Fold waxed canvas as little as possible, because hard creases can crack the wax.
- Before storage, make sure everything is completely dry.

## 5. Common Mistakes

| Mistake | What Happens | Fix |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Washing a waxed coat in a machine | Wax strips out, water resistance gone | Rewax (Section 2) |
| Drying boots by a fire | Leather shrinks and cracks | Replace; prevention only |
| Over-oiling | Leather goes soft and sags | Let it rest and don't oil again for 6 months |
| Storing damp | Mold and mildew | Brush off, dry, and see `REPAIR_POLICY.md` for exclusions |
