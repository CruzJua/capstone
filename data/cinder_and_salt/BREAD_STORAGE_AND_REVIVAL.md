# LOAF CARE, STORAGE & REVIVAL GUIDE

**Cinder & Salt Bakery** — *Wild Yeast. Heritage Grain. Honest Craft.*

Our loaves contain only flour, water, and sea salt, with no preservatives. That is what makes them honest, and it also means they need a little care.

## 1. The Science of Staling: Why Refrigeration Is Forbidden

Commercial bread uses mono- and diglycerides and chemical preservatives. Cinder & Salt sourdough contains only flour, water, and sea salt.

- **Starch Retrogradation:** Staling is not simply drying out. When bread cools after baking, the gelatinized starch molecules gradually recrystallize and push moisture out of the crumb, which makes it firm and leathery.
- **The Refrigerator Trap:** Refrigeration temperatures (35°F–42°F / 1°C–5°C) **accelerate retrogradation by up to 300%**. *Never store fresh artisan bread in the refrigerator.*
- **Why Sourdough Lasts Longer:** Natural acids from wild fermentation slow mold and retain moisture, which is why a high-hydration loaf like our Country Sourdough (78% hydration) usually stays pleasant for 4 to 5 days.
- **Crust Moisture:** Crust softens as moisture migrates outward. That is normal, and it can be reversed (see Section 3).

## 2. Storage Timeline: Counter vs. Freezer

### Counter Storage
- **Days 1–2:** Place the loaf cut-side down directly on a wooden cutting board at room temperature. The cut face protects the crumb and the crust stays crisp.
- **Days 3–5:** Transfer the loaf into an unbleached linen bread bag or brown paper sack. Avoid airtight plastic bags, which soften the crust and encourage mold.
- **Location:** Keep away from the stove, direct sun, and humid spots. Ideal room temperature is 65°F–72°F (18°C–22°C).

### Freezer Portioning (Up to 3 Months)
1. **Slice first:** Slice the whole loaf on the day you buy it, or the day after.
2. **Separate:** Place parchment squares between slices so you can take out only what you need.
3. **Seal:** Place in a sealed freezer bag and press out as much air as possible.
4. **Freeze:** Store at 0°F (-18°C), and label with the date.
5. **Use:** Toast slices straight from frozen at medium heat. For a whole frozen loaf, thaw on the counter for 2 to 3 hours, then use the refresh technique below.

*Note: Enriched pastries (croissants, Danishes, morning buns) are best eaten the same day. Freeze them within 24 hours and reheat at 350°F (177°C) for 8 to 10 minutes.*

## 3. The 5-Minute Oven Refresh Technique

1. **Preheat Oven:** Set the oven to **375°F (190°C)**.
2. **Moisten the Crust:** Run the entire unsliced stale loaf under cold tap water for 3 to 5 seconds, or spritz generously with a water bottle. Do not soak.
3. **Bake:** Place the wet loaf directly on the center oven rack for **6 to 8 minutes**.
4. **Rest:** Allow the loaf to rest for 5 minutes before slicing. Steam gelatinizes the interior crumb, which reverses some of the retrogradation, while the crust crisps.

**Tips**
- Use this for loaves up to about 3 days old. Beyond that the bread is best sliced and toasted, or made into croutons, breadcrumbs, or panzanella.
- Half loaves: cut-side down, 4 to 5 minutes.
- Revived bread is best eaten the same day, because it will stale faster the second time.
- **Mold:** If you see any mold, discard the whole loaf. Do not cut around it.
