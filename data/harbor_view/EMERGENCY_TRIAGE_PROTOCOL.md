# PROTOCOL: Urgent Care Triage & Emergency Hospital Referral

**Harbor View Veterinary Clinic** — *Compassionate Medicine. Modern Care. Lifelong Companionship.*

If you believe your pet is in danger, please do not wait. **Call us at (555) 438-2323 on your way**, or go straight to the 24/7 emergency hospital if we are closed.

## 1. Color-Coded Triage Classification System

Incoming urgent cases are assessed immediately upon arrival, by medical need and not by arrival order:

| Triage Category | Patient Clinical Presentation | Action / Clinical Pathway |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **RED (Critical Emergency)** | Respiratory distress, collapse, active seizures, GDV / bloated abdomen, unresponsiveness, uncontrolled bleeding, rectal temperature at or above 104.0°F (40.0°C) or below 99.0°F (37.2°C) with weakness | Immediate transfer to treatment bay; oxygen cage & IV catheter stabilization |
| **YELLOW (Urgent Priority)** | Male cat straining to urinate (suspected blocked cat), acute toxin ingestion, ocular injuries, deep lacerations, repeated vomiting, rectal temperature of 103.0–103.9°F (39.4–39.9°C) | Exam within 30 minutes; pain relief & diagnostic ultrasound triage |
| **GREEN (Stable Outpatient)** | Mild ear infection, chronic skin itching, broken toenail without arterial bleed, limping with bearing weight | Scheduled same-day work-in appointment slot |

**Notes**
- A pet in any category can be re-triaged upward at any time. If your pet's condition changes in the lobby, tell reception immediately.
- Wait times for GREEN patients can be long on busy days. We will tell you honestly what to expect, and you may go to another hospital at any time.

## 2. Life-Safety Warning: Blocked Male Cat

Male cats can develop a urethral obstruction. Signs include repeated trips to the litter box, crying, straining with little or no urine, licking the genital area, hiding, or vomiting.

- **This is a life-threatening emergency.** Potassium builds up in the blood, which can cause heart rhythm abnormalities and kidney failure within **24 to 48 hours**.
- **Never wait overnight** to see whether it passes. Do not give any home remedies or human medication.
- **If we are open:** Come immediately and call from the car. The cat is triaged YELLOW at minimum and RED if he is weak, cold, vomiting, or collapsed.
- **If we are closed:** Go directly to the Regional Animal Specialty & Emergency Center (24/7 ER), **(555) 948-3700**.
- **Treatment:** Typically includes sedation, catheter relief of the blockage, IV fluids, and monitoring, and overnight hospitalization is generally required. We provide an itemized estimate whenever possible before treatment, without delaying emergency stabilization.

## 3. Common Toxin Ingestions (YELLOW at Minimum)

Chocolate, xylitol (sugar-free gum), grapes/raisins, lilies (cats), rodenticide, and human medications (ibuprofen, acetaminophen) are all urgent. Bring the packaging. You may also call the ASPCA Animal Poison Control hotline (consult fee applies) at 888-426-4435.

## 4. Outpatient Clinic Limitations & 24/7 Emergency Transfer

Harbor View is a day practice and does not offer overnight intensive care monitoring:

- **Transfer Criteria:** Patients requiring continuous IV fluid titration, arterial blood gas audits, blood transfusions, or overnight oxygen therapy are stabilized and transferred.
- **Designated Regional Referral Partner:** *Regional Animal Specialty & Emergency Center (24/7 ER)* — Phone: **(555) 948-3700**. Transport records and radiographs are transferred digitally.
- **Hours:** Urgent walk-ins are accepted during clinic hours only. Cases arriving less than 30 minutes before closing may be referred to the ER if they cannot be stabilized and discharged safely.

## 5. Referral Handover SOP

1. **Stabilize:** Place an IV catheter, provide oxygen, pain relief, and warmth as indicated.
2. **Call Ahead:** The attending veterinarian phones the ER doctor at (555) 948-3700 with a verbal case summary, including the triage color, vitals, medications given, and expected arrival time.
3. **Send Records Digitally:** Exam notes, labs, radiographs, medication logs, and vaccine history. A printed copy goes with the guardian.
4. **Transport Guidance:** Advise the guardian on safe transport. A second person should ride along if possible. Carriers for cats, and dogs with suspected back injury on a flat board or blanket stretcher.
5. **Financial Transparency:** The guardian is told that the referral hospital sets its own fees. Harbor View charges only for services provided here.
6. **Follow-Up:** A technician calls the ER within 24 hours for an outcome update, and the veterinarian contacts the guardian to offer a recheck visit.

## 6. Communication Standards

- Speak calmly. Provide a clear next step in the first 30 seconds of any call.
- Never diagnose by phone. Advise, triage, and refer.
- When in doubt, treat it as an emergency.
